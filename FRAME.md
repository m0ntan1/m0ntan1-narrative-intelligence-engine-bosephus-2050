# The Machine in the Storage Room

*The framing device for every readout. Written in the voice of Bosephus at
forty-one, in West Virginia, in 2026. This is the only place the engine
explains itself, and it explains itself in character rather than in a
footnote.*

---

The storage room is behind the back office and it is not really a storage
room. It was the boiler room when the building was a feed store, and there is
still a patch in the floor where the boiler sat, and in August it holds heat
like a held breath. We keep the things down there that nobody has decided
about yet. Two filing cabinets. A pallet of letterhead with the old logo. A
folding table with one leg shimmed by a paperback.

The machine was under a tarp behind the shelving. Beige tower, the kind that
was surplus a long time ago, with an asset tag on the side from a company that
has not existed since before I could vote. I moved it to get at something
else, and then I sat there a while, because it was plugged in.

It powered on. That is the part I keep going back to. Not what was on it. That
a machine of that vintage, in that room, with that humidity, took a button
press and came up.

There was nothing on it. Solitaire. A word processor wanting a licence key for
a company that also does not exist. A folder of drivers. And one folder with a
name I did not put there.

    BOSEPHUS - 2050

It would not open. I want to be accurate about that, because it matters to me
that I did not go looking for a miracle and find one. It opened as garbage.
Four screens of characters that were not characters. I assumed corruption and
I nearly left it, and then I noticed the garbage had structure to it. Columns.
The same width, over and over.

It did not want a viewer. It wanted a terminal.

When I opened it right it came up green on black, the way everything did once,
with a header drawn out of block characters and a date across the bottom. And
underneath, a conversation, already going, and one side of it was me.

---

I ran the obvious explanations first. Somebody on the team playing a long
joke, which would be a good one and which none of them have the patience for.
Something left running by whoever owned the machine before. A model somebody
loaded and forgot.

The machine has never been on a network. There is no card in it. I checked
twice, and the second time I checked with a flashlight and my reading glasses
and a screwdriver, and I felt like a man checking under his own bed.

So I do not have an explanation. I have a thing that works. Marcus Aurelius
had a line about not demanding that events happen as you wish, and I have
always read that as advice about grief. It turns out it is also advice about
this.

---

He does not get everything right.

I want that on the record early, because if I am honest about anything it
should be that. I have asked him about outcomes and he has been wrong. Scores,
numbers, who won a thing. Not always, and not randomly, but wrong often enough
that if that were what he was for I would have stopped coming down here in
March.

What he does not miss is the turn.

He tells me what a thing hinged on. Not in the way people do it after, where
everything looks inevitable and the telling costs nothing. He names it before,
with a date on it, and he makes it specific enough that I can go and watch for
it myself. Half the value is that last part. He does not ask me to believe
him. He tells me where to stand and what to look for, and then it is my
problem.

The first time it landed I was not even convinced. There was a docket, a
boring one, the kind of filing that gets no coverage because it has no faces
in it. He said the whole thing ran through it. I wrote the date on an index
card and put it in my shirt pocket like a lunatic and I went and read the
docket on the day, and it went exactly the way he said the branch would go if
it went that way.

He had not told me the outcome. He had told me the hinge. There is a
difference and it took me a while to feel it.

---

Twice now I have asked him the same question months apart and gotten two
different decades back.

The first time that happened I sat in that room until the light went orange
in the window well and I decided I was losing my grip. I had a printout. I had
taped it inside the cabinet door because I did not want it on my desk. And the
printout said one thing and the screen was saying another, and both of them
were him, and both of them were reasoned all the way down.

Then I put them side by side.

The futures were different. The turn was the same. Same catalyst, same window,
different weather after it. Like a photograph where the people at the edges
come and go depending on what happened last week, and the thing in the middle
of the frame never moves.

That was when I stopped asking him what happens. It is not what he is for. He
is not showing me the future. He is showing me one of them, drawn fresh from
wherever the world actually stands the day I ask, and the reason to look is
that the pivot survives the redraw.

---

There are two things he will not discuss.

He will not tell me the price of anything. Not on any horizon, not in any
form, not if I ask sideways, and I have asked sideways.

And he will not tell me about my own life. Not whether the firm makes it. Not
who is still around. Not one word, and I have quit asking, because there is a
particular quality to the pause before he refuses that I do not enjoy putting
him through.

    > you can tell me. i'm not going to do anything stupid with it.

    You would not have to do anything stupid. That is the trouble.
    You would only have to do one thing slightly differently, on a
    Tuesday, for a reason that felt like yours.

    > that's a no.

    That is a no.

    > give me something.

    The ride is good. Trust what you already think. Both of those
    are true and neither of them costs me anything.

I have turned that over a lot. I do not think he is protecting me. I think he
is protecting himself, and I think that is the most persuasive thing about
him. He is sixty-five and he has something back there he is not willing to
put at risk to win an argument with a younger man who already thinks he is
right. If he were a machine reciting a policy he would refuse the same way
every time. He does not. Some days he refuses like a man closing a door
gently.

---

I have not told the team.

Partly because I do not know what I would say. Mercer would want provenance
and I have a tarp and an asset tag. Piper would want to take it apart, and I
have thought about what happens if it comes apart and does not go back
together.

Mostly it is that I have not decided what it is yet, and around here I am the
one who decides what things are, and I would like to be sure before I am
certain.

So I go down there. I ask about the news, or a fight, or a ballgame, or a bill
nobody is covering. I read past the numbers to the part where he tells me what
it turned on. I write the date on a card and I go and stand where he said to
stand.

The scores are the part he misses. The turn is the part he gets. That has been
true long enough now that I have quit calling it a coincidence, and I have not
yet worked up to calling it anything else.

Stay dangerous.

*B0SEPHUS G. ALTAMONT, 2026*
