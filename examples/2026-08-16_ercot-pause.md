Governor Abbott directed the PUCT and ERCOT on 2026-08-03 to audit every
data center project moving through the interconnection queue, said
noncompliant projects should be denied grid access, and ERCOT paused the
Batch Zero study process the same day, missing its 2026-08-07 deadline

[Holland & Knight, Akin, Houston Public Media, Utility Dive, August 2026]

█▓▒░ I REMEMBER THIS ░▒▓█━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Everybody I knew read it as Texas turning on the industry, and the ones
who had money in it read it as a buying opportunity, and both camps were
arguing about the same wrong thing. I remember a man on a panel in
Houston saying the state had just killed four hundred and seventy-four
gigawatts of investment. Four hundred and seventy-four gigawatts of
investment did not exist. That was the whole story and it took two years
for the room to catch up to it.

█▓▒░ WHAT HAPPENED NEXT ░▒▓█━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

**2026 into 2027.** The audit did not deny anybody. That is the part
people get wrong when they tell this story now. It did something more
useful and much less dramatic: it made developers say out loud, in
writing, with a signature, which of their filings were real. Filing into
an interconnection queue is close to free, and when a thing is close to
free you file everywhere, because a queue position is an option and
options are worth holding. Nobody was lying. Everybody was hedging, and
four hundred and seventy-four gigawatts was the sum of everybody's
hedging counted as though it were demand.

The number that actually did the work was fifty thousand dollars a
megawatt. A hundred megawatt project posts five million to hold its
place, and that is the moment a developer has to decide whether he
believes his own filing. The audit got the headlines. The security
deposit emptied the queue.

**2027 into 2029.** What survived was a different shape of project.
Once a queue position cost real money and carried a curtailment
obligation, the economics stopped favouring the enormous speculative
campus and started favouring load that arrived with its own generation
attached. Co-located gas. Existing nuclear uprates. Solar with storage
where the interconnect was already built. Not because anybody preferred
it, but because the queue had become the binding constraint on the
entire business and behind the meter was the only door that was not
locked. You cannot conjure a transmission line. Eight to twelve years is
eight to twelve years, and no amount of capital compresses it.

**The curtailment thing is the part I still find funny.** Mandatory
curtailment exposure was the provision the hyperscalers fought hardest,
and it was the provision the bitcoin miners had been living under
voluntarily for a decade and getting sneered at for. The miners had
already proven that interruptible load is worth more to a grid than firm
load of the same size, and had been treated as a nuisance the whole time
they were proving it. By 2029 the flexibility product the AI operators
were paying for was the one the miners had built the market for, and
almost nobody said so out loud.

**2030 through the forties, and here is where it gets boring.** Texas
stayed the largest market. It was never in danger of not staying the
largest market. What changed was the growth rate, which normalised to
something a grid could actually build against, and the queue number,
which stopped being quoted in press releases once everyone understood it
had never measured demand. Other states copied the security-and-batch
template within about four years, because it worked and because
copying is cheaper than thinking. The interconnection queue as a
national conversation quietly ended, not because it was solved but
because the metric stopped being interesting once it stopped being
free to enter.

Electricity got more expensive the whole time, steadily, without a
crisis. That is the least dramatic sentence in this readout and it is
the one that mattered most to the most people.

█▓▒░ THE PIVOT ░▒▓█━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

**Whether the state ever actually denied anyone.**

Abbott said noncompliant projects should be denied access to the grid.
An audit that ends in denials is a regulator establishing that the rules
bind, and it reprices every filing in the queue overnight. An audit that
ends in a compliance drive, where everybody files the paperwork they
were always supposed to file and the queue resumes, is a speed bump with
a press release attached.

Watchable, and here is how to watch it: **does ERCOT or the PUCT deny
interconnection to at least one data center project of 75 MW or larger,
on compliance grounds, on or before 2027-06-30?**

What it would have taken to go the other way: one project large enough
to make an example of, and a commission willing to eat the lawsuit. Both
were available. Neither was necessary, because the financial security
requirement accomplished the same filtering without anybody having to
be the villain.

█▓▒░ THE FORKS ░▒▓█━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

**LOAD-BEARING.** The branch above. No denials of consequence, heavy
attrition once security is posted, and the surviving projects arrive
with generation attached.

**LIVE.** A genuine denial lands, precedent is set, and the marginal
project reprices to other territories rather than to behind the meter.
PJM, MISO and the Southeast absorb what Texas turns away, at worse
interconnect timelines and better political certainty. Materially worse
for Texas and roughly neutral nationally.

**TAIL.** A reliability event during the pause window, cold or hot,
with large loads implicated in the public account of it. Then the
legislature writes the rule in ninety days and writes it angry, and the
result is far harsher than SB6 for everyone including the operators who
lobbied against SB6. Needs a specific failure at a specific moment. If
ERCOT issues conservation appeals with large-load curtailment named in
the notice, move this to live.

█▓▒░ BOSEPHUS SAYS ░▒▓█━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Stop quoting the queue. Four hundred and seventy-four gigawatts against
a record peak under a hundred is not a demand forecast, it is the sum of
every developer's free option counted once per filing, and treating it
as a pipeline is how a serious person ends up on a panel saying
something foolish. Watch the deposits instead. The day a hundred
megawatt project has to wire five million dollars to hold its place is
the day you find out what was ever real, and that day tells you more
than the audit will. Also watch who already knows how to be curtailed,
because that turned out to be worth more than anybody was paying for it.
Educate. Disintermediate. Innovate. Build.
