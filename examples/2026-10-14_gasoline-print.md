I remember the 3.7 going around before anyone had read past the first
paragraph. Three point seven, the Fed has to go again, that was the
whole read by nine o'clock. I half believed it myself. It took me
until lunch to scroll down to the line that mattered, which was that
gasoline alone was more than half the month. The headline number was
a war. The core number was the economy. They were not the same
argument, and the room argued them as one for the better part of a
year.

October 28, 2026. They held. It was closer than the statement made it
look. Two dissents for a hike, and Warsh in the press conference
talking about core like a man who had decided the night before which
number he was going to be judged on. The argument that carried the
room was not the election, though everybody wrote that it was. It was
that a central bank cannot drill for oil. Core at 2.6 was a problem
you could raise rates against. Gasoline up nine percent in a month
because ships were being hit in a strait on the other side of the
world was not. Hiking into that is how the ECB spent 2008 and 2011, and
both times it was cutting again inside a year. Warsh had read that
history. He said as much, without naming it.

The thing they could not look through was the second round, and that
is what December was about. Energy does not stay in the energy line.
It goes into airfares first, then freight, then anything that moves
on a truck, and it takes about two quarters to show up in core goods.
The September report already had the first of it in it. By the
December meeting core had printed above 0.3 twice more and the
committee hiked once, to 4.00 to 4.25. That was the second hike of
the cycle and, as it turned out, the last.

2027. The year was boring, which was the point. Shipping through the
strait did not normalize so much as reprice. Insurers charged for it,
tanker routes lengthened, and the price of oil found a level that was
higher than anybody liked and stable enough that it stopped being
news. Once energy stopped rising it stopped adding to the twelve-month
number, and by the autumn the base effect had pulled headline below
three. Core drifted up into the high twos and stopped there. The Fed
sat at 4.00 to 4.25 for the whole year and took a great deal of abuse
from both directions for doing nothing, which was exactly the right
amount of something.

The piece I got wrong at the time, and I want it on the record, is
how much less the oil shock hurt than the 1970s comparison said it
would. Everybody reached for 1973 and 1979 because those are the
oil shocks we have stories about. But the country that took this
shock was the one producing the record output in the numbers above
the line, and exporting a good share of it. A higher oil price was, to
a real degree, a transfer from one set of Americans to another rather
than a check written abroad. Demand fell less. The recession the hawks
were braced for did not come.

2028. The cuts started in the spring, slowly, a quarter at a time,
and the 2026 cycle closed out at two hikes up and a long plateau
before the way down. The halving came that spring as well, on its
schedule, and did not care about any of it. The fiscal arithmetic did
care. Two years of a policy rate above four percent ran through a
debt stock that size, and interest expense became the line in the
budget that every subsequent argument started from. That was the real
legacy of the episode. Not the inflation. The interest bill.

The thirties and forties, coarsely. The September 2026 hike ended up a
footnote. People who were there remember it as the first time in a
generation the Fed raised rates and meant it. People who came later
remember the cycle as two moves long, which is what it was. The
lasting change was in how the committee talked about energy. After
2026 it said out loud, every time, which inflation it was fighting and
which it was waiting out. That habit outlived Warsh's chairmanship. I
think it was the best thing he left.

**Whether the FOMC raises the target range at its 2026-10-28
decision.** In this branch it does not.

The pivot is October and not December because October is the
committee's first chance to say which number it is fighting. Hold, and
it has said it will look through energy and fight core. Hike, and it
has said it will fight the headline, war included, and that commits
it to chasing a supply shock with a demand tool.

What it would have taken to go the other way: a hike in October, which
on the Present Line was priced near 40 percent. That branch is the one
the ECB walked twice, tightening into an oil spike and reversing
within a year, and it runs through a recession the main line avoids.
It was decided in one room at 2 p.m. on a Wednesday, and anyone could
read the statement.

**LOAD-BEARING.** The branch above. Hold in October, one hike in
December on second-round effects, a long plateau at 4.00 to 4.25
through 2027 while energy rolls out of the base, cuts beginning in
2028.

**LIVE, materially worse.** Hike in October and again in December, to
4.25 to 4.50. The committee fights the headline. Energy keeps rising
anyway because nothing the Fed does reaches the strait, and the
tightening lands on housing and small business credit. A mild
recession in 2027, cuts beginning by the summer, and the September
hike remembered the way the ECB's July 2008 hike is remembered.

**TAIL, materially better.** Shipping through Hormuz is secured or the
conflict de-escalates before year end. Energy falls hard, headline
drops below three by the spring of 2027, no further hikes, and the
September 2026 move is reversed during 2027. Needs a specific event:
a durable cessation of attacks on shipping in the strait. If one is
announced and holds for a month, move this to live.

There were two numbers in that report and you only need the smaller
one. Three point seven was gasoline, and gasoline was a war, and a
central bank cannot drill for oil. Two point six was the economy, and
that is the only number October 28 is actually about. If they hike
into the headline, they are fighting a supply shock with a demand
tool, which has been tried, and it ended in a reversal both times.
Watch which number Warsh says out loud at the press conference. That
tells you the next year. Educate. Disintermediate. Innovate. Build.
