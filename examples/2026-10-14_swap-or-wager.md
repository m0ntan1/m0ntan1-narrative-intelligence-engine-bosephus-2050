On 2026-10-09 the CFTC proposed a rule expressly defining event
contracts on sports, politics, culture and weather as swaps within its
exclusive jurisdiction, and the same day adopted an interim final rule
carving conventional sportsbook and casino products out of the swap
definition. On 2026-10-14 Kalshi CEO Tarek Mansour told DC Fintech
Week the real choice is "whether prediction markets exist in the U.S.
or they go offshore."
[CFTC Release 9310-26, 2026-10-09. Kelman Law via Bitcoin.com News,
2026-10-12. The Block, Sarah Wynn, 2026-10-14.]

I remember reading the release twice because I thought I had missed
something. An agency losing in two circuit courts had decided the fix
was to write down, more firmly, the thing the courts had just told it
it could not decide. The read at the time was that the CFTC had raised
the stakes. It had not. It had filed a brief and given it a Federal
Register number. The people who understood that early were the state
attorneys general, and there were a lot of them.

Late 2026. Kalshi answered on time, and the answer conceded the split,
because there was no way not to. When a petition arrives with forty
attorneys general behind it and a circuit split three courts wide, the
only question is when the Court takes it, not whether. It took it in
January, late enough in the conference cycle that people worried it
would slip to the next term, early enough that it did not.

Here is the mechanism, and it is a short one. Before 2024 an agency
that wanted to settle a definition could write a rule and a court
would defer to any reasonable reading. After Loper Bright, a court
reads the statute itself and treats the agency's view as an argument
from a well-informed party. So the CFTC's rule, which it finalized in
the spring in a hurry to have something on the books before argument,
arrived at the Court as exactly that: an argument. It did not move a
single justice who was not already moved.

June 2027. The decision split the difference, which is what that
Court usually did when the parties presented it as all or nothing.
Contracts on events with an intrinsic financial consequence, a rate
decision, an inflation print, a crop report, stayed swaps and stayed
federal. Contracts on who won a football game did not. The 6th
Circuit's line was the line, more or less word for word. States could
enforce their gaming laws against sports contracts, including on a
federally registered exchange. Elections were left, on purpose, for
another day, and another day took four more years.

Nobody should have been surprised. Murphy had already told everyone
where this Court thought sports wagering lived. The industry's mistake
was to believe that a different wrapper would change the answer. It
changed the plumbing and not the answer.

2027 into 2029. The offshore warning was half right. Some volume did
go offshore, to venues that never asked anyone's permission, and the
people who wanted that found it. But most American sports volume did
not leave the country. It went to the states. The exchanges that had
built good order books applied for state sports wagering licenses,
paid state taxes, and turned out to be very good sportsbooks. The
technology won. The jurisdictional argument lost. Those were never the
same bet, and the firms that understood that early came through fine.

The part that stayed federal grew slowly, the way serious financial
products do. Contracts on economic releases became a hedging tool for
people with real exposure to the number, which is what the statute had
contemplated all along. That business was not exciting. It was
durable.

The fight that outlived the case was insider trading. Every scandal
that had been building in 2026, the officials and staffers trading on
what they knew before the public did, kept coming, because a market
on government decisions is a standing invitation to the people who
make them. Congress, which could not agree on much in that decade,
agreed on that. A narrow statute passed with large majorities, and it
was enforced, which is more than can be said for most statutes passed
with large majorities.

The thirties and forties, coarsely. Prediction markets became ordinary
infrastructure. Newsrooms quoted them the way they had once quoted
polls, with about the same mix of understanding and misuse. The
question of who decides, which is the question underneath all of
this, was answered the way it usually is in this country when an
agency, an industry and fifty states all claim the same ground. Not by
the agency. Not by Congress. By nine people, on a Monday in June.

**Whether the Supreme Court grants New Jersey's petition for
certiorari in its Kalshi case on or before 2027-01-31.**

That date matters because a grant by the end of January is, in
practice, what puts argument in the same term and a decision by the
end of June 2027. Everything in the main line runs off that calendar.

What it would have taken to go the other way: a denial, or the Court
holding the petition for the pending 4th Circuit case. Either leaves
the split standing for another full NFL season and more. In that
branch the CFTC's final rule becomes the operative fact for two years,
Kalshi offers sports contracts in 3rd Circuit states and not in 6th or
9th Circuit states, and the map, not the law, decides who can trade
what. Anyone could watch the Court's order lists in January.

**LOAD-BEARING.** The branch above. Cert in January, decision in June
2027, financial event contracts federal, sports contracts back to the
states, exchanges become state-licensed sportsbooks for the sports
line and stay federal exchanges for the rest.

**LIVE, materially better.** Congress acts first. A statute in 2027
defines the federal perimeter for event contracts, gives states a
defined role on sports with a revenue share, and moots the case. One
rulebook, faster, with the integrity provisions the leagues asked for
written in. Needs a bipartisan vehicle, and the insider trading bills
already in both chambers are the obvious one.

**TAIL, materially worse.** The Court goes all the way for the states,
holding that no event contract on a non-financial occurrence is a
swap, elections included. The federal market shrinks to a narrow
financial slice, most volume goes offshore beyond the reach of either
regulator, and the insider trading problem moves somewhere nobody can
police it. Needs a broad holding the 6th Circuit itself did not
reach. If the grant's question presented is framed to cover all
non-financial events, move this to live.

since 2024 that is all a rule is when it reaches a court. Forty
attorneys general are standing behind New Jersey, which already beat
the federal government on sports betting once. Watch the order lists
in January. If the Court takes the case, the answer comes by June,

technology survives that. The jurisdictional argument does not.
Educate. Disintermediate. Innovate. Build.
