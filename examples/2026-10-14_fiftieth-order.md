- On 2026-10-09 FERC accepted PJM's definition of a large load (50 MW

October 2026. The order expired on the Friday and nobody pulled a
data center onto its diesel. The heat broke. The alert came down. If
you were grading the week on whether the lights stayed on, it was a
nothing week, and that is how most of the press graded it.

The count did not stop. That is the whole mechanism, so I will say it
plainly. A reserve margin that is thin because load grew faster than
supply cannot be fixed on the timescale of a news cycle. Gas turbines
were sold out years ahead. Transmission takes eight to twelve years.
So the one federal tool that worked in days was the emergency order,
and a tool that works gets used. Section 202(c) had been written for
wars and hurricanes. By the end of 2026 it was the capacity market
nobody voted for.

Winter 2026 into 2027. The January cold snaps did what January cold
snaps do, and the 2027 orders started in the same week the 2026 ones
had. People stopped writing about each one. That is how you know a
thing has become structure: the coverage stops.

The FERC ruling of 2026-10-09 is the part I misjudged at the time. I
read it as PJM losing. It was PJM being told where the decision
lived. Curtailing a retail customer is a state power, and FERC said so
in a sentence. That moved the fight out of one federal docket and into
thirteen state commissions and the District, and state commissioners
answer to people who pay residential bills and vote in November. The
midterms put the electric bill on the ballot in every PJM state that
had a data center fight, and the commissioners read the returns the
same way everybody else did.

First half of 2027. Pennsylvania finished what it started. The final
order kept the core of the tentative one: new large loads that had not
brought their own new capacity got curtailed first, and a data center
could not call itself critical load to get out of it. The industry
fought the drafting, won some definitions, and lost the principle.
That template travelled. Not uniformly, because thirteen commissions
never do anything uniformly, but the shape was the same everywhere it
landed within about two years.

Here is what nobody expected, and it is the part worth remembering.
The developers took the deal. Not because they were persuaded.
Because the alternative was a five year queue, and a five year queue
in a business that measures obsolescence in eighteen months is a
refusal with extra steps. So the price of a fast interconnection
became interruptibility. Connect now, come off the grid first. The
backup fleet that had been sitting idle, the thirty five gigawatts DOE
counted, stopped being insurance and became the grid's reserve.

The split that made it work was inside the buildings. Training runs
can pause. Inference cannot. Operators learned to sort their own load
into the part that could take a curtailment call and the part that
could not, and they bought firm power only for the second part. It
took most of 2027 and 2028 for that to become ordinary practice. It
is the kind of thing that sounds obvious once someone has paid to
learn it.

The fight that followed was local, and it was about air. Backup
generators running under emergency waivers are diesel and gas
burning in places that were zoned for warehouses, not power plants.
The county hearings of 2028 and 2029 were louder than anything at
FERC. Some of those fights the neighbors won, and in those counties
the backup fleet converted to batteries and gas turbines with real
permits, slower and costlier.

2029 into the early thirties. The turbines ordered in 2025 through
2027 arrived on roughly the schedule the manufacturers had said, which
surprised people who had stopped believing schedules. New supply
caught some of the load. The annual count of emergency orders came
down, not to the old handful, but into the teens, where it stayed.

The thirties and forties, coarsely. The grid that came out of it had
two classes of large customer, firm and flexible, priced very
differently. That is not new. Aluminum smelters ran on interruptible
tariffs for most of the last century. Compute just rediscovered the
arrangement at larger scale and called it something else. No great
blackout came out of this branch. The thing everyone feared in October
2026 did not happen. The thing almost nobody noticed, that the
emergency had quietly become the operating mode, was the story.

**Whether the Pennsylvania PUC issues a final order on or before
2027-06-30 that keeps the curtail-first provision for new large loads
lacking sufficient new capacity.**

Pennsylvania is the hinge because FERC put curtailment in the states'
hands on 2026-10-09, and Pennsylvania was the first large PJM state
already holding a written rule when it did. The first final rule
becomes the template the others argue against.

What it would have taken to go the other way: the commission
softening curtail-first into pro rata curtailment, or dropping it
under industry pressure during the comment period. In that branch
the states never pick up the tool FERC handed them, the federal
emergency order stays the only lever, the count keeps climbing, and
the cost of the reserve lands on households instead of on the loads
that caused the need for it. Anyone reading the Pennsylvania docket
in the spring of 2027 could watch it go one way or the other.

**LOAD-BEARING.** The branch above. Emergency orders become routine,
the states write curtail-first into tariffs one commission at a time,
interruptibility becomes the price of a fast connection, and the
count falls into the teens once new turbines arrive around 2029.

**LIVE, materially better.** PJM refiles a version built with the
states rather than over them, FERC accepts it in 2027, and one set of
rules covers all thirteen states and the District inside two years
instead of five. Same destination, less patchwork, and the backup
fleet gets paid as a resource on uniform terms rather than drafted
under emergency waivers.

**TAIL, materially worse.** PJM declares an EEA 3 and sheds firm load
before any state rule is final, in winter 2026 to 2027 or summer 2027.
Rotating outages in an election aftermath produce a moratorium on new
large-load interconnection in one or more PJM states, and the build
moves to wherever the moratorium is not. Needs a specific event: an
EEA 3 with firm load shed. If PJM sheds firm load, move this to live.

Do not read the weather. Read the docket number. Fifty emergency
orders by the middle of October, against fourteen the year before, is
not an emergency, it is a market design that nobody voted on, and FERC
told you on October 9 that the people who get to vote on the fix are
state commissioners. Watch Pennsylvania's final order next spring. If
curtail-first survives, the data centers pay for their own reserve by
agreeing to come off the grid first. If it does not, your electric
bill pays for it, and the emergency stays the operating mode.
Educate. Disintermediate. Innovate. Build.
