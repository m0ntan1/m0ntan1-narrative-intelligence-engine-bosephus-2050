Why Narrative

The catalyst identification problem in modern LLMs, and the fix

The problem is not knowledge. It is tense.

Ask a current model what happens next and it returns the safest available object: a hedged, multi-factor summary that cannot be wrong because it never commits. Regulatory clarity, adoption curves, macro conditions. Every driver named, none of them load bearing, nothing dated. The output is unfalsifiable, so it cannot be graded, so nothing downstream of it can improve. That is the catalyst identification problem. The model will discuss causes at length and refuse to nominate one.

Why it happens. Two forces stack. Calibration training rewards appropriate uncertainty, and forward-looking assertions are exactly where that training bites hardest. Then the request itself is malformed: asking for the single correct future is a search for an object that does not exist, and under that incentive the rational move is to widen the claim until it is safe. The mush is not a failure of intelligence. It is a correct response to a bad question.

Why prompting harder does not work. "Be specific." "Commit to one answer." "No hedging." These fight the trained behavior head on and lose. What comes back is compliance theater, a confident-sounding paragraph with the same non-committal structure underneath, or a refusal. The deeper issue is that you are asking the model to operate the weakest grammar the language has. Future statements arrive pre-hedged in every human language, and plenty of languages have no dedicated future tense at all. None are missing a past.

The move: change the tense, not the instruction.

Fix a vantage in 2050. Ask what he remembers 2026 turning into. Same model, same retrieved record, different grammatical mood. Recollection licenses detail in a way prediction never does, because nobody demands justification for a specific inside a memory. Say it was raining and the folder was green and the meeting ran long, and a listener just follows along. Put those same specifics in a forecast and the listener leans back. The stance shifts on both ends of the exchange, and none of it requires anyone to lie.

Why this produces catalysts specifically. This is the part that earned the design rather than merely permitting it. Memory is catalyst-shaped by default. When a person recounts how something turned out, they do not recite a distribution. They tell you what it hinged on. Retrospection compresses causality into turns, because that is structurally what a story is. So the named, dated hinge falls out of the format for free. I never had to engineer the model into nominating a pivot. Nominating one is what remembering is.

What it costs, and the fence around it. A well-told story persuades whether or not it is true, which makes the fiction the dangerous component here, not the clever one. Two structures contain it. The seam: everything above it is retrieved and carries a source, everything below it is constructed, and the line is dated. The ledger: every pivot is written down with a date and a binary resolution, and it is graded on whether it fired before anyone looks at the outcome. Fantasy buys the specificity. The ledger charges for it. Neither half is safe alone.

The whole thing in one line. A model will not name a catalyst in the future tense. It names one automatically in the past tense. The engine is a tense change plus a scoreboard.
